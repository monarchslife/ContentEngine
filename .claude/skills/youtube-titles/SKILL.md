# YouTube breakout titles

Read `docs/SOP.md` and `data/swipe-file.json` in full before writing anything. The SOP is the method; the swipe file is the only evidence you may cite.

For the user's idea:

1. **Proposition.** One sentence on what the video genuinely delivers, the intended viewer, and anything you had to assume because the idea didn't say it.
2. **Three titles, three different mechanisms.** Rank 1 is the primary: the mechanism best supported by the swipe file and best matched to the video's substance. Ranks 2 and 3 are alternative hypotheses using genuinely different mechanisms, not rewordings.
3. For each title give:
   - Mechanism (taxonomy key, or "new hypothesis" with a provisional label if nothing fits).
   - Precedents: swipe-file IDs with the exact title, views and age copied from the file. Never invent or round numbers. Thumbnail-text entries are unverified packaging.
   - Evidence level, derived strictly: no precedent = new hypothesis; one = single observation; two or more = repeated examples (no controls or baselines yet). Nothing higher; the corpus has no controls.
   - SOP section 7 scores (0-2 on six criteria), total out of 12 and its band.
   - Audit, what the video must deliver for the title to be honest, and remaining uncertainty.
4. **Why these three differ** and how confident the ranking is.

Rules: learn the mechanism, never copy a precedent's wording; no claims, numbers or timeframes the idea doesn't support; describe viewer psychology as hypothesis, not fact; a score is a decision aid, never a probability of going viral.
